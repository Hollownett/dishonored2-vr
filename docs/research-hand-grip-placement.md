### 1.2 HIGGS

[V] The hand is not moved. The object is translated so the **closest mesh point to the palm ray lands on the palm position**; the object's rotation relative to the hand stays whatever it was at grab time:

* Pick the point: `GetClosestPointOnGraphicsGeometryToLine(triangles, palmPos, palmDirection, ...)` ([math_utils.cpp L2176](https://github.com/adamhynek/higgs/blob/93bf67b1bc4c4a11a20ccaef0d5012781d0d7eee/src/math_utils.cpp#L2176)): for every triangle, the closest point to a ray from the palm along the palm normal, scored `directionalWeight*along^2 + lateralWeight*lateral^2`, front faces only (normal dot palm direction <= 0).
* Apply it: `desiredNodeTransform.pos += palmPos - ptPos; desiredNodeTransformHandSpace = inverseHand * desiredNodeTransform;` ([hand.cpp L1544-1547](https://github.com/adamhynek/higgs/blob/93bf67b1bc4c4a11a20ccaef0d5012781d0d7eee/src/hand.cpp#L1544)). Only a translation; the object's rotation is the one it had.
* Authored override: object "grab nodes" give a fixed `palmTransformCurrent * palmOnObjToObj` (the grab-node comment says they take priority). Palm point and direction come from config (`PalmPosition`, `PalmVector`, [config.cpp L204-206](https://github.com/adamhynek/higgs/blob/93bf67b1bc4c4a11a20ccaef0d5012781d0d7eee/src/config.cpp#L204)); the values are in the ini, **[U]**.

So in HIGGS: the grab position is **where the hand touched**, the axis/spin are **whatever the hand had**, the radius never appears (the palm sits on the skin), and there is no separate cylinder logic.

### 1.3 Authored-point systems (SteamVR/Alyx style, Hurricane, Auto Hand, Meta)

* SteamVR [V]: an object has a `SteamVR_Skeleton_Poser` whose pose snapshot stores a hand **position/rotation relative to the object**; on attach with `SnapOnAttach`, `objectToAttach.transform.position = hand.TransformPoint(pose.position)` and the offset is stored ([Hand.cs L475-537](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/steamvr_unity_plugin/blob/056c82369d78f253af8cefcae9b289efd69bd960/Assets/SteamVR/InteractionSystem/Core/Scripts/Hand.cs#L475)); the finger pose is the main pose lerped toward open/closed by finger curl ([poser L282-330](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/steamvr_unity_plugin/blob/056c82369d78f253af8cefcae9b289efd69bd960/Assets/SteamVR/Input/SteamVR_Skeleton_Poser.cs#L282)). Easing: `snapAttachEaseInTime` with a curve ([Hand.cs L1211-1228](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/steamvr_unity_plugin/blob/056c82369d78f253af8cefcae9b289efd69bd960/Assets/SteamVR/InteractionSystem/Core/Scripts/Hand.cs#L1211)).
* Alyx [U]: per-prop `prop_handpose` markers placed on each grab spot (a ladder uses one per rung), with an "extend box" that defines where the hand may grab and an auto-grip option ([ladders](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Half-Life:_Alyx_Workshop_Tools/Level_Design/Ladders), [prop_handpose](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Prop_handpose); wiki text via search snippet only).
* Hurricane VR [V from docs]: grab points are `HVRPosableGrabPoint` with a `HVRHandPoser` (primary pose plus blendable per-finger poses) ([Hand Posing](https://cloudwalkingames.gitbook.io/hurricanevr/hand-posing)); `PhysicsPoserFallback` = "If the grab type is snap and no pose resolves, tries dynamic grabbing instead"; `ConsiderGrabPointAngle`; `GrabBehaviour` (hand goes to the object, or the object is pulled to the hand); `PalmCenterOfMass` ([HVRGrabbable](https://cloudwalker2020.github.io/HurricaneVR-Docs/api/HurricaneVR.Framework.Core.HVRGrabbable.html)). The store page and Hurricane's overview advertise an "Alyx-style dynamic hand pose solver" as the fallback when no pose is authored ([overview](https://cloudwalkingames.gitbook.io/hurricanevr), details **[U]**).
* Auto Hand [V from docs]: automatic poses by default; custom `Grabbable Pose` picks "the pose closest to the hand's relative position and rotation on grab"; **Advanced Grabbable Pose** "constrains the grip to a line or radius for objects like a bat handle or steering wheel", with `Angle` and `Range` "around and along the local up direction", a `Center Object` pivot and `Use Invert Pose` (180 deg flip) ([custom poses](https://earnest-robot.gitbook.io/auto-hand-docs/auto-hand/custom-poses)). This is the same slide-and-spin freedom as Marrow's cylinder. The palm transform is "the point and forward direction of the grab" ([hand](https://earnest-robot.gitbook.io/auto-hand-docs/auto-hand/hand)).
* Meta ISDK [V]: `CylinderGrabSurface`: radius = "the distance from the cylinder's axis to the original grip position", set automatically; cap points define the slide range; arc offset/length limit the spin; `CalculateBestPoseAtSurface` projects a wanted pose onto the nearest valid pose ([reference](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/reference/unity/v68/class_oculus_interaction_grab_grab_surfaces_cylinder_grab_surface/)). Without a surface "your hand snaps to the position of the pose" ([authoring](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/unity-isdk-create-handgrab-poses-mac/)). Recommends scaled pose copies at 0.70 and 1.30 hand size.

### 1.4 Answers to the five alignment questions

* **Which point/axis aligns:** the object's cylinder axis goes through the hand's grip point, parallel to the fist axis. The palm normal points at the axis, and the palm surface is `r` away from it. Marrow encodes this as a hand-in-handle transform per radius ([V data above]); Meta states it as "radius = axis to original grip position".
* **Radius:** shifts the hand 1:1 away from the axis (Marrow data: offset = r + ~46 mm) and sets how closed the fingers are. HIGGS has no radius: the palm sits on the mesh.
* **Spin about the axis:** free in Marrow (joint with friction), free in Auto Hand's advanced pose (angle limit), arc-limited in Meta, and "whatever it was at grab" in HIGGS.
* **Position along the axis:** where the hand touched, clamped to an authored range (Marrow `limit`, Auto Hand `Range`, Meta cap points); HIGGS: closest mesh point.
* **Tilt of the axis relative to the fist:** limited by `swingRotationLimit` (30-60 deg) in Marrow; poses are blended across pitch/roll/yaw samples so the fingers stay plausible while the hand tilts.

## 3. How finger poses are produced at a grab

* **Authored, blended by radius and tilt (Bonelab, SteamVR, Hurricane, Auto Hand, Meta):** section 1. The finger pose is stored; the controller finger input blends toward open (`BlendOpenPose`, SteamVR `fingerCurl` lerp between open and closed, Hurricane per-finger masks/weights, Meta `Free/Constrained/Locked` per finger: "Constrained: the finger won't move past its position in the pose").
* **Per-finger sweep (HIGGS):** [V] `FingerCheck(i)` moves each finger's recorded start position by `palmToPoint` ("where the hand would be if it was already holding the object") and calls `GetIntersections` ([hand.cpp L1475-1497](https://github.com/adamhynek/higgs/blob/93bf67b1bc4c4a11a20ccaef0d5012781d0d7eee/src/hand.cpp#L1475), [math_utils.cpp L1989](https://github.com/adamhynek/higgs/blob/93bf67b1bc4c4a11a20ccaef0d5012781d0d7eee/src/math_utils.cpp#L1989)). Each finger has three recorded arcs (**tip, outer, inner**) from [`finger_curves.cpp`](https://github.com/adamhynek/higgs/blob/93bf67b1bc4c4a11a20ccaef0d5012781d0d7eee/src/finger_curves.cpp) (up to 201 samples per finger and curve, `{curl value, angle, length}`, built by animating the hand open to closed and measuring). The triangles near the touch point are intersected with the arcs in the finger's plane; the **smallest non-negative angle per arc** wins, converted back to a curl value by table lookup; the **largest curl value (least curl) of the three arcs** is used. A negative angle (finger already inside the object) opens that finger to 1.0. **No hit closes the finger.** Result clamped to a minimum 0.2 (open fraction), "some min value to not overcurl the finger". No mesh at all: all fingers 0.9.
* **Thumb (HIGGS):** same test; if the standard thumb curve misses or is negative, a second **alternate thumb curve** (index 5) is tried and used if it hits, or if both miss ([L1507-1520](https://github.com/adamhynek/higgs/blob/93bf67b1bc4c4a11a20ccaef0d5012781d0d7eee/src/hand.cpp#L1507)).
* **Application:** finger values are fed to a speed-limited animator (`AdvanceTransform` toward the lerped closed/open pose with linear and angular speeds, [finger_animator.cpp](https://github.com/adamhynek/higgs/blob/93bf67b1bc4c4a11a20ccaef0d5012781d0d7eee/src/finger_animator.cpp)). HIGGS also pre-closes the fingers to `SelectedCloseFingerAnimValue` when an object is only **selected** (hand near it) ([hand.cpp L2723](https://github.com/adamhynek/higgs/blob/93bf67b1bc4c4a11a20ccaef0d5012781d0d7eee/src/hand.cpp#L2723)).
* **Thumb (authored systems):** the thumb is part of each pose (Marrow `thumb1` quaternion + `thumb2/3`; in the data the thumb flexes 52/84 at 5 mm and 4/23 at 78 mm). Meta's `Free/Constrained/Locked` is per finger. No public source describes a separate "thumb opposition" solver.

## 4. Keeping the hand out of the object

* HIGGS: the palm **is** placed on the nearest surface point and the fingers stop at the first arc hit, so the hand is outside by construction. Bounds: mesh triangle accuracy (box colliders are not used for this; the visible mesh is).
* Marrow: authored. The hand-origin offset is radius + ~46 mm for cylinders ([data table above]); penetration beyond that is the pose author's problem. The grab joint uses `projectionMode`/`projectionDistance` in `HandJointConfiguration` [S] to avoid drift.
* Auto Hand: finger **tip radius** "will act as a bumper for automatic grabbing" ([finger component](https://earnest-robot.gitbook.io/auto-hand-docs/auto-hand/hand/finger-component)); the palm transform is placed at the palm centre with the forward axis "facing away from the hand".
* Meta touch grab: no description of penetration handling; known issue that large objects can lock to the hand ([touch hand grab](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/unity-isdk-touch-hand-grab-interaction/)).

## 5. Bottle-like objects specifically

No public source states "bottle: grip at X". What is public:

* Marrow: bottles/cups would be a `CylinderGrip` (or `BarrelGrip`/`TargetGrip`) with an authored `limit` (grab range along the axis). The community asset pack ships `CylinderGripSmall` (swing limit 60 deg, radius keys down to 4.9 mm) and `CylinderGripLarge` ([assets](https://github.com/ItzHanchik/MarrowSDK-Extended/tree/47a704955b782b7db8add146e48d52b7a18d0b60/Data/HandPose)). The grab point on the body is **where the hand touched**, within that range.
* HIGGS: the closest mesh point to the palm ray, so the body wherever the hand is.
* Auto Hand/Hurricane/SteamVR: authored grab point (a mug handle, a bottle neck) plus the closest saved pose to the hand's relative pose on grab.
* Real-world prior [I]: bottles are held on the body below the shoulder, long axis along the fist, thumb opposing on the far side; the neck sticks out past the index/thumb side.

Take-aways [I]:
1. Every public general solution uses **either a ray to find the touch point (and a normal), or shape tests per finger**. None needs the visible mesh at runtime except HIGGS at grab time.
2. Surface contact for a **passive** hand (resting on a table) is not documented in any of them; Bonelab and Auto Hand rely on the physics hand colliders and the controller finger input, with the finger bend clamped or stopped by the contact.
3. Colliders, not render meshes, are what the live queries use (Meta, Bonelab, Auto Hand). Layer masks decide what counts as a surface (Bonelab `fingerColCheckMask`, `hoverLayerMask`; Meta bounds collider).

## 8. For this mod

Current state: `publish_held_object()` ([src/dxgi_probe.cpp](../src/dxgi_probe.cpp)) puts the mesh centre of the held entity on the palm point (`origin = palm - X*centre.x - Y*centre.y - Z*centre.z`, with a fixed object basis `X=-up, Y=left, Z=forward`) and gives the engine a pose through `NativeHeldObject`. `palm` is `hand_bind_grip`'s interior palm centre, so a bottle's axis passes **through** the palm and not at one radius outside it. `src/finger_ik.hpp` already does per-finger closure against **one** `HeldShape` (rounded box / capsule; `classify_held` fits a cylinder to an AABB; `handle_shape` puts a capsule at `palm_in*(radius+0.01)`); it is only fed when a grab or hold is active. Placement and finger shape must come from the same numbers, so the fixes below are written to share one fit.

### A. Placing a held bottle in the palm

1. **One cylinder fit, used for both placement and fingers.** Run `classify_held` on the held entity's bounds (already read at `model+0x1840` with axes at `+0x186c`) once at pickup and keep `{axis a (unit, object frame), half length h, radius r, mesh centre c}`. The object's long axis `a` is the cylinder axis; do not assume the mesh's local Z. If `classify_held` returns a box (residual > 10%), use the largest extent as the axis and `r = min(extent_y, extent_z)` for placement only.
2. **Axis along the grip forward axis, offset by the radius.** With the grip frame `{f = forward, l = left, u = up}` and `n = palm_normal` (the `hand.palm_in` direction in grip space, as `handle_shape` computes): the axis line is `P(s) = palm + n*(r + skin) + f*s`, `skin` = the existing +0.01 m (Marrow data: hand origin sits at r + ~46 mm from the axis, which includes the palm interior depth; keep `skin` as a knob in `build/held-offset` next to the existing x/y/z knob). The object origin is `P(s) - R*c` where `R` is the object-to-world rotation of step 4.
3. **Slide to the lower third for bottles.** Choose `s` so the grip point is at fraction `t` of the body measured from the base: `t = clamp(t_touch, 0.15, 0.45)` where `t_touch` is the projection of the palm at the moment of pickup onto the axis (HIGGS closest point / Marrow "where the hand was", clamped like Marrow's `limit`), and the default `t = 0.30` when the pickup has no touch point (the native HoldObject path). Base = the end with the larger cross-section, or the end nearer the floor at pickup. Reasoning: real bottles are held below the shoulder; this is a design choice, not a source statement **[I]**.
4. **Orientation: axis to the fist, spin from the hand, with an ease.** Rotate the object so `a` aligns with `+f` (or `-f`, whichever keeps the object's current world-up tilt smallest at pickup; keep that sign for the whole hold). Keep the roll about the axis that the object had relative to the hand at pickup (HIGGS preserves rotation; Marrow lets the cylinder spin freely), and let later hand twist (about `f`) rotate it with the hand. Blend from the pre-pickup pose to the target over ~0.12 s (SteamVR `snapAttachEaseInTime`; HIGGS/Auto Hand `Maintain Grab Offset` are instant). Clamp the tilt away from the fist to the Marrow swing limit, 30 deg for a large radius, 60 deg for a small one.
5. **Finger shape = the same capsule.** Feed `finger_contact` the fitted cylinder after step 4 (`frame` = the object's mesh centre in world, axis `+f`, `half = {h,0,0}`, `radius = r`; `seat_shape` already builds this from `held_cylinder_now` and the seat), so the fingers close on exactly the cylinder that steps 2-4 place. Targets from the Marrow data for a sanity check: radius 25 mm gives index j2/j3 near 57/33 deg and thumb near 38/42; radius 78 mm gives 18/20 and 4/23. If `finger_contact`'s result differs by more than ~15 deg per joint at those radii, trust the contact (it is geometric) and adjust `thickness`.
6. **Thumb opposing.** The thumb is chain 0 with full angles {15, 40, 55} in `finger_full_degrees`. Its hinge is set so it closes toward the index knuckle, so for a cylinder on the palm-normal side it already closes across the shape; ensure the thumb is tested against the same capsule, not given a free default. If the capsule is too wide for the thumb to reach (r above ~0.05 m), leave the thumb at its contact value; do not force a closure. Cap every finger at ~0.85 of its full angle to keep clear of the skin (HIGGS minimum open fraction 0.2, "to not overcurl the finger").
7. **Do not move the hand to the object.** The engine hand is tracked; place the object relative to the tracked palm (the current approach, and the Marrow/Hurricane default for a held cylinder). Only when the controller is far from the pickup (the existing >0.6-0.7 m auto-release rule) fall back to the pull.
8. **Check list for the headset session:** (i) bottle axis parallel to the fist; (ii) bottle surface touching the palm skin, not the interior point; (iii) body is gripped below the shoulder; (iv) fingertips stop on the glass, thumb opposes; (v) rolling the wrist spins the bottle about the axis; (vi) release and re-grab keep the same grip point if the hand hasn't moved. Simulated checks do not establish this.

### B. A general finger-on-anything solution (replace "one held shape" by "shape set")

9. **Query nearby surfaces, not only the held thing.** Build a small `Shape[]` per hand per frame from three sources, and make `finger_contact` take the **minimum signed distance over the set** (the SDF union; `shape_distance` already returns signed distance, so this is a one-line change in `clearance`): (a) the held or grabbed shape (today's input); (b) loose props near the palm: the registry scan pattern of `small_creature_on` (list every ~250 ms, test every frame) but over rigid props, classified with `classify_held` from their bounds; (c) **static world** from a palm sphere sweep along `palm_in` with the engine cast `c89bc0` (see [native-world-trace.md](native-world-trace.md); radius ~0.03 m, length ~0.10 m, filter `0xc43e`, drained in the Stage0 scope, one frame of latency). The hit point and normal become a slab for the fingers: a rounded box with `half = {0.4, 0.4, 0}` across the surface and `radius = 0.02`, its frame placed 0.02 m below the hit point along `-normal`, so its top face is the surface and a finger cannot end up behind a zero-thickness sheet.
10. **Contact only clamps; add a resting curl for surfaces.** Today `grip_curls` takes `min(wanted, contact.finger)` so an open controller hand never curls. Bonelab/Auto Hand keep the fingers driven by the controller and let contact stop them, so a relaxed hand on a table has flat fingers (**[U]** in the public sources for passive touch). Make that explicit: when the palm is within ~0.03 m of a surface and the hand is slow (< 0.3 m/s), raise `wanted` toward `min(0.35, contact.finger)` for the four fingers (Auto Hand `Grip Offset` 0.14 is "a slight bend"), so the hand lies naturally on the surface; release it over `finger_ik_tau`. This is a design proposal **[I]**.
11. **Cost budget.** Section 6 shows the cheap designs: one ray (Bonelab `GenericGrip`, Auto Hand), per-grab triangle work (HIGGS), or iteration-capped shape tests (Meta). Budget here: <= 2 sweeps/frame/hand (palm + one fingertip refinement every other frame), <= 4 nearby props per hand, the existing 15 evaluations per finger in `finger_contact`. Skip the whole path when no hand is near anything (palm > 0.25 m from every candidate).

### C. Physical hands that move things on a table

12. **Hand bodies: kinematic, not dynamic.** The engine has its own character controller and the hands are visual, so copy HIGGS, not Bonelab: one **keyframed hknp box per hand** (palm box about 0.09 x 0.025 x 0.10 m in grip space, rounded 0.01 m, optionally a second finger box that is enabled only when the fingers' contact says they extend past the palm, like Bonelab's `fingersCol` toggle), driven to the tracked palm each frame by a velocity setter (the grab motor's 0xc85dc0 drives existing dynamic bodies; that it drives a new keyframed body, and how to create the body at all, is untested and is the first spike **[I]**) with `v = (target - position)/dt`, `|v| <= 4 m/s` and `|omega| <= 8 rad/s`; if the required speed exceeds the limit, **teleport** the body to the target (HIGGS `ApplyHardKeyframeVelocityClamped`), never let it sweep through props at high speed. Collides with dynamic props only: not the player capsule, not static world, not NPC bodies (NPC contact stays on the melee path), never the held object. Disable it entirely while a physical grab is active (HIGGS `shouldDisableCollision`).
13. **Table safety: never press a prop into a static.** Reuse the palm sweep from step 9c: when the sweep reports a static surface within 0.01 m, remove the component of the hand body's velocity into that surface (so a hand lowered onto a bottle on a table stops instead of squeezing it), and draw the visible hand clamped to the plane (Bonelab `SkeletonHand` displaces the drawn hand from the physics hand by up to 0.18 arm lengths; Hurricane `HandSweep` / Auto Hand `Max Follow Distance` return the hand when stuck). Without this, a kinematic body with infinite mass squeezes a dynamic prop against the table and Havok launches it (the failure Auto Hand, Hurricane and Bonelab all work around with force caps and joints; **[I]** that it applies here).
14. **Mass and strength.** A kinematic hand has infinite mass, so enforce strength in software like Bonelab's muscle clamp: read the touched body's inverse mass from the body record if available, and ignore (or ghost) bodies heavier than ~10 kg (a hand cannot push a car), scale the allowed hand speed by `min(1, 2 kg / m)` for lighter-but-not-light bodies. The existing grab motor already caps at 10 m/s and 5 rad/s; keep the table values far lower (4 m/s). Wake sleeping props only above 0.05 m/s relative speed. Use a short no-collision window (0.1 s) after release (already planned in `research-physical-interaction.md`).
15. **Verification plan.** Add sim scripts that (a) slide the hand over a bottle on a table at 0.5, 1.5 and 3 m/s, (b) rest a palm on a table with bottles beside it for 10 s, (c) press down on a bottle. Watch for: bottle velocity > 3 m/s after any contact (launch), bottle penetration into the table, jitter (position variance > 2 mm while at rest), and hand tunnelling. Then the headset for feel. Starting numbers above (4 m/s, 10 kg, 0.03 m sweep radius, 0.35 resting curl) are **guesses to tune**, not source values.

## Gaps worth stating plainly

* There is no public Bonelab/Boneworks documentation or source for grip alignment or the physical hand solver; section 1.1 and 7 are field-level and data-level only. The exact radius/pry weighting, `limit` use and joint drive values are **[U]**.
* Half-Life: Alyx hand posing has no primary public source I could read; the Valve wiki was blocked.
* Hurricane VR and Auto Hand algorithms are paid code; their docs give settings, not math.
* No source documents how any of them pose fingers on a surface the player is only *resting on*.
